About

Philologists in love with the written word

Who is Ana Todorović-Radetić

Since 2016 she has run the Prevodioci Libra translation agency in Belgrade, translating texts of every level of complexity and managing numerous translation and proofreading projects.

Teaching experience

She has run adult courses for Banca Intesa and Ball Packaging Europe, as well as for the Serbian Chamber of Commerce, the Civil Aviation Directorate, the Centre for Business Education and the Ministry of Health.

She uses mind maps, the technique popularised by Tony Buzan, to teach grammar, and created the educational website grammarmindmaps.com for that purpose.

Ask a question
What does proofreading and copy editing include?

A thorough, systematic review of your material, so the final text is clear, readable and linguistically correct:

  • formatting – page layout, margins, line spacing, font, headings and subheadings;
  • typos – spelling slips and stray spaces;
  • orthography – punctuation, capitalisation, abbreviations, compound words, transcription of foreign names;
  • grammar – agreement (person, gender, number and case) and verb patterns;
  • syntax – conjunctions, punctuation in complex sentences, word order and clarity;
  • vocabulary and style – redundancy, repetition and unidiomatic expressions.
What is the difference between copy editing and proofreading?

The Serbian word lektura comes from the Latin legere – to read. A copy editor works deep in the text: spelling, grammar, style and meaning. Proofreading (Serbian korektura, from corrigere – to correct) is the final polish after editing and typesetting: removing remaining typos, double spaces, incorrect hyphenation and technical slips.

Today the two are usually combined into a single service, as that is the only way to get a fully finished text.

Do you proofread texts in foreign languages?

Of course. We proofread every language we translate into and from – some thirty, including English, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Slovenian, Czech, Romanian and Arabic. If needed, we translate your text first and then proofread the translation.

Do you proofread texts written in dialect?

Yes. Dialects have no standardised spelling rules, so besides fixing slips and punctuation, the editor makes sure characteristic expressions are written consistently. With these texts, close communication with the author is especially important.

What kinds of texts do you proofread?

No limits: novels, short stories, poetry, plays, diaries and travel writing; essays, bachelor’s and master’s theses, doctoral dissertations, articles and studies; brochures, manuals, textbooks and guides; blogs, websites, ads and subtitles. We also proofread PowerPoint presentations and Excel spreadsheets.

Do you proofread specialist texts?

Yes – law, economics and marketing, medicine and pharmacy, computing, mechanical and civil engineering, ecology, tourism, the arts, sport and many other fields. Specialist texts are assigned to editors who specialise in that field.

How can I see what was changed?

We work in Word with Track Changes switched on, so every correction is highlighted and clearly marked. You receive two versions: one with visible changes and a “clean” version with all changes accepted, ready for further work or publication.

Will proofreading change my style?

No. The editor removes redundancy and obvious repetition, splits overlong sentences and fixes unclear constructions, but doesn’t “go too far” or spoil your authentic style – which matters especially with literary texts. If you need deeper work on structure and style, that is substantive editing, which we always do in consultation with the author.

Is one read-through enough?

Never. The first pass is for typos and formatting, the second for spelling and grammar, the third for style. Each pass also catches anything missed before, and at the end we review the final version with all changes accepted once more.

Does proofreading include preparing the text for print?

The editor also formats the text (layout, margins, line spacing, font, headings, alignment), so the final version is print-ready. For publications with photos and complex layouts we work with graphic designers, and for web content with SEO specialists.

How do I send my text, and in what format?

The easiest way is by email, to prevodiocibg@gmail.com. We usually receive Word documents, but we also work on PowerPoint presentations, Excel spreadsheets and other formats. We return the edited text by email by the agreed deadline.

Do you sign a non-disclosure agreement (NDA)?

Yes. If required, we sign a non-disclosure agreement (NDA) before receiving your document, so your text is protected before we even open it. We can sign our standard agreement or one prepared by your company or legal team.

The NDA binds every editor and collaborator working on the project. We use your texts only for the agreed job, keep them no longer than agreed and delete them once the work is done. This is standard practice for unpublished manuscripts, theses before their defence, legal, business and tender documents, and medical records.

How long does proofreading take?

It depends on length, content, complexity and how much editing is needed. Shorter texts are often ready the same day; for longer material we give a precise estimate – and we always meet the agreed deadline.

Do you issue invoices?

Yes. Companies and sole traders receive an invoice, and individuals a fiscal receipt, which we can send electronically together with the edited text.
